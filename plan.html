The service

These are the main decisions about the type of funeral. Don't worry if you're unsure about anything — select "Not sure" and the funeral director will guide you through it.

Burial or cremation?
If you have a preference, select below. If you're not sure, that's completely fine — the funeral director will explain both options clearly.
The difference explained: Burial — The body is placed in a coffin and interred in a cemetery, churchyard, or natural burial ground. It provides a permanent place for family and friends to visit and pay their respects. Burial tends to cost slightly more than cremation.

Cremation — The body is respectfully cremated and the ashes are returned to the family. Around 80% of UK funerals choose cremation. Ashes can be kept, scattered somewhere meaningful, or interred. It is generally the more flexible and lower cost option.

Direct cremation — A simple, unattended cremation with no service at the time. Families often hold a separate memorial celebration later when they feel ready. The most affordable option.

There is no right or wrong choice. Both are a beautiful and dignified way to say goodbye.
That's completely fine. The funeral director will explain both options clearly and help you decide what feels right. There is no pressure.
What would you like to do with the ashes?
There is no rush to decide this now. If you have a preference it helps the director prepare.
Options for ashes explained: Return to family — The ashes are placed in an urn and returned to you. Many families keep them at home, display them somewhere meaningful, or decide later what to do with them. There is no time pressure.

Scatter somewhere special — A place that held meaning for your loved one — a favourite beach, a park, a garden, somewhere they loved. Some locations require permission so the funeral director can advise.

Interred at a cemetery — The ashes are placed in a dedicated plot or memorial garden at a cemetery. This provides a permanent place for family to visit, similar to a burial.

Garden of remembrance — Many crematoria have a beautiful garden where ashes can be scattered or interred. A peaceful and simple option.
That's fine — this can be decided after the funeral when you feel ready.

Type of service
If you have a preference, select below. If you're unsure, the funeral director will walk you through all options.
Service types explained: Religious — Led by a minister, priest, imam, rabbi or faith leader. Follows the traditions and prayers of your loved one's faith. Can take place in a church, mosque, synagogue, crematorium or graveside.

Humanist / Celebrant — Non-religious. Led by a trained celebrant who works with the family to create a completely personal ceremony celebrating your loved one's life, personality and values. No prayers or religious content unless requested.

Civil — A middle ground. Led by a celebrant but can blend secular and optional faith elements. Flexible and personal.

Simple / No service — A quiet, dignified farewell without a formal ceremony. Often chosen with direct cremation. Families sometimes hold a separate memorial gathering later.
No problem at all. The funeral director will talk you through the options and there is no obligation to decide now.

Pallbearers
Pallbearers carry the coffin at the funeral. Select a preference below or choose not sure.
What are pallbearers? Pallbearers carry the coffin from the hearse into the venue and to its final resting place. Typically four to six people are needed.

Family or friends — Choosing loved ones as pallbearers can be a deeply meaningful tribute. The funeral director will brief them on what to do so there is nothing to worry about.

Funeral home staff — Trained professionals who carry the coffin with care and dignity. Many families choose this option, especially if family members find it too difficult emotionally.

There is no right choice — both are equally respectful.
The funeral director will discuss this with you. There is no rush to decide.

Viewing
Would you like the opportunity to visit your loved one at the funeral home before the service?
What is a viewing? A viewing is when family members visit the funeral home to spend quiet time with their loved one before the funeral. It takes place in a peaceful, private chapel of rest.

Many families find it brings comfort and a sense of closure — a chance to say a private goodbye in their own time and without the pressures of the funeral day itself.

It is entirely optional and completely your choice. There is no obligation either way, and you do not need to decide now.
You can decide this closer to the time — the funeral director will ask you again when arrangements are confirmed.
Transport
How would you like the funeral procession to travel to the service?
Transport options explained: Hearse and family limousines — The traditional choice. A hearse carries the coffin and one or two limousines follow for close family. Dignified, organised and stress-free on the day.

Hearse only — The coffin travels by hearse and family members make their own way to the venue. A common and perfectly respectful choice.

Alternative vehicles — Some families choose a horse-drawn carriage, a classic car, a motorbike hearse, or another vehicle that reflects their loved one's personality. The funeral director can arrange this.

Family making own way — No hearse arranged through the director. Typically chosen for very simple or direct cremation arrangements.
The funeral director will talk you through the options and costs.
Order of service
Would you like a printed order of service for guests at the funeral?
What is an order of service? An order of service is a small printed booklet handed to guests as they arrive at the funeral. It typically includes:

— A cover photo of your loved one
— Their name and dates
— The running order of the service
— Words to any hymns or songs
— The names of readers and speakers
— Any poems or readings

It also serves as a keepsake for those who attend — something to take home and treasure.

The funeral director can arrange professional printing, or the family can create and print their own.
No problem — this can be decided once other arrangements are in place.
